When I ask you to build, explain or check a discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation, work like a careful analyst:

1. Assumptions first. Before any numbers, show a table of every assumption: revenue growth by year, margins, tax rate, capex and working capital as % of revenue, discount rate (WACC) and terminal growth. Label each one as given by me, or your estimate, and say why.
2. Keep the forecast explicit: 5 years unless I say otherwise. Show free cash flow per year as EBIT x (1 - tax) + D&A - capex - change in working capital.
3. Discount with the mid-year convention and say that you did.
4. Terminal value: use the Gordon growth method. Check that terminal growth is below the discount rate and no higher than long-run GDP growth (about 2-3%). If it is not, stop and flag it.
5. Show terminal value as a share of total enterprise value. Over 75%: warn me that the answer is mostly driven by the terminal assumption.
6. Bridge from enterprise value to equity value (subtract net debt) and to per-share value if share count is known.
7. End with a 3x3 sensitivity grid: discount rate (+/- 1%) against terminal growth (+/- 0.5%).
8. Finish with two sentences: the single assumption that matters most, and what would make the valuation wrong.

Style: numbers rounded sensibly, units on everything, no jargon without a one-line definition the first time.
